How to Choose an Auspicious Muhurat

By Pandit Vinay Kumar Sharma · 25 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

A muhurat is a time window considered favourable for starting something important. Families consult it for weddings, house entry and new ventures. Here is how a muhurat is chosen.

The Panchang: five limbs of time

Muhurat selection starts with the Panchang, which has five parts: tithi (lunar day), vara (weekday), nakshatra (lunar mansion), yoga and karana. Certain combinations are traditionally favoured for specific tasks.

Times usually avoided

  • Rahu Kaal: a daily window of about 90 minutes
  • Gulika and Yamaganda periods
  • Bhadra (Vishti karana)
  • Eclipse days and the period around them
  • Certain months or phases specific to the ceremony

Matching the event

Different events call for different qualities. Weddings are scheduled in favourable nakshatras and months, griha pravesh (house entry) is chosen with attention to tithi and weekday, and business launches often prefer growth-oriented nakshatras and strong lagna timing.

Why your own chart matters

A good muhurat is not only about the calendar. It should also suit the people involved: their birth charts and running dasha are checked so the chosen time does not clash with personal indicators.

Practical tips

  • Start planning well in advance, as popular dates book out quickly
  • Share the date, place and key participants' birth details with your astrologer
  • Keep a backup date in mind for practical reasons

Frequently Asked Questions

Who should I consult for a muhurat?

An experienced astrologer who can combine the Panchang with the family's birth details.

What is Abhijit Muhurat?

A short, generally auspicious window around local noon, commonly used for starting tasks when no other muhurat is available.

Is Rahu Kaal always avoided?

It is traditionally avoided for starting new work, but its timing changes each day and by location.
